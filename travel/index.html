Travel notes · immigration

Before you travel

Entering Kenya

For most guests, entry to Kenya is managed through the Electronic Travel Authorisation (eTA) system. Because requirements vary by nationality, please check your eligibility carefully and apply in good time.

  • Ensure your passport is valid for at least 6 months.
  • Have your travel itinerary and accommodation details ready.
  • Plan your airport pickup or first-night transport in advance.
  • Allow extra time for Nairobi traffic.
Official Kenya eTA website How to apply

Helpful local notes

Once you arrive, Nairobi is very manageable with a little planning. Uber and Bolt are widely used for getting around, and Safaricom shops in the major malls are wonderful for a local SIM card and easy connectivity.

We recommend confirming accommodation, arrival transport and event-day movements with a little time to spare.

Please verify independently. Some travellers — including certain East African nationals and other exempt nationalities — may not require an eTA. Entry rules change, so please rely on official government guidance for the latest requirements before you travel.
Arriving by air

Jomo Kenyatta International Airport

Most international guests will arrive at Jomo Kenyatta International Airport (NBO), south-east of the city centre along Mombasa Road. The reception venue, Emara Ole Sereni, is also off Mombasa Road.

Pre-arrange your transfer with one of the transport providers below.

Money

Currency & payments

Kenya’s currency is the Kenyan shilling (KES). Mobile money — M-Pesa — is used almost everywhere, and cards are widely accepted at hotels, malls and larger restaurants.

General guidance only — please check with your bank about card use abroad.

Weather

Nairobi in December

December days in Nairobi are typically warm and bright, with cooler mornings and evenings thanks to the city’s altitude. Occasional showers are possible.

Pack a light layer or wrap for the evenings.
